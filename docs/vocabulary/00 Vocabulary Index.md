# Tier 0 — Vocabulary

Read these once, slowly. Everything else in graphs is built on these words.

The goal is not to memorize definitions. The goal is that when an interviewer says
*"the graph is undirected and may contain cycles"*, your brain immediately fires off
a list of consequences: *which algorithms are legal, which bugs I'm about to hit, what
the edge count can be.*

⏱️ Budget: 1 day.

---

## Read in this order

1. [[01 What Is a Graph|What Is a Graph]] — two sets, and the modeling habit that carries every problem
2. [[02 Vertex and Edge|Vertex and Edge]] — the surrounding terminology, and the `V`/`n`, `E`/`m` notation
3. [[03 Directed vs Undirected|Directed vs Undirected]] — the most consequential distinction; one line of code
4. [[04 Weighted vs Unweighted|Weighted vs Unweighted]] — the fork that picks BFS vs Dijkstra vs Bellman-Ford
5. [[05 Paths Walks and Cycles|Paths Walks and Cycles]] — why path length counts edges, not vertices
6. [[06 Cyclic vs Acyclic and DAGs|Cyclic vs Acyclic and DAGs]] — the most important special structure in interviews
7. [[07 Connectivity and Components|Connectivity and Components]] — the single most common bug in graph problems
8. [[08 Degree|Degree]] — sources, sinks, leaves, and why Kahn's algorithm is obvious
9. [[09 Self-Loops and Multi-Edges|Self-Loops and Multi-Edges]] — the edge cases that quietly break working code
10. [[10 Trees|Trees]] — connected + acyclic + `V-1` edges, any two imply the third
11. [[11 Dense vs Sparse|Dense vs Sparse]] — how this silently decides your complexity
12. [[12 Implicit Graphs|Implicit Graphs]] — a huge fraction of interview problems, and people miss them
